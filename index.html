Procurement × GCC/GBS: Aligning for Value

Procurement and GCC/GBS leaders should run one procurement agenda with one value scorecard, explicit decision rights and a legal design that keeps pace with the GCC's growing role. Without this, the GCC becomes a cost-per-FTE story while procurement chases savings alone.

The market79% of CPOs expect 2026 to be harder, 61% saw savings targets rise, and only 11% have scaled AI. Talent, data and late business engagement are the binding constraints.
The opportunityIndia's 2,117 GCCs (2.36 million people) are moving from back-office transactions to owning sourcing analytics, category work, risk and procurement technology.
The alignmentA signed CPO–GCC charter, a RACI with signing authority kept in the contracting entity, a joint Finance-validated scorecard, shared governance and cross-boundary careers.
The missing piecesDecision rights, a shared savings definition, clean data, AI governance and a legal/tax review of the GCC's functional profile.
The legal safeguardsAvoid permanent-establishment and transfer-pricing exposure by keeping the GCC in a prepare-and-recommend role unless deliberately redesigned; use Budget 2026's expanded safe harbour or fast-track APA; prepare now for DPDP duties (from May 2027) and the new labour codes.
The roadmapDiagnose (30 days), pilot (90 days), scale (one year), with two hard gates.
Success and course-correctionMeasure on one Finance-validated scorecard (value, efficiency, effectiveness, GCC maturity, risk, data and AI); review monthly, quarterly, half-yearly and annually; when results fall short, diagnose first, then tune, realign, re-scope or restructure, with legal checks at each step.

What this means in practice

02

Procurement as a value creator

Procurement creates value when it is measured on business outcomes, not on negotiated price variance. Leading functions now report a balanced value scorecard to the CFO and BU heads.

Cost (P&L)
Should-cost, demand management, spec simplification, index-linked pricing
Measure: savings vs. market index, booked into budget ("budget-hit" savings)
Cash / working capital
Payment-term harmonisation, supply-chain finance, dynamic discounting
Measure: DPO change, cash released
Risk & resilience
Dual sourcing, supplier financial monitoring, tariff and sanctions scenario plans
Measure: revenue-at-risk covered, time-to-recover
Growth & innovation
Early supplier involvement, joint development, supplier-led ideas
Measure: revenue from supplier-enabled products, time-to-market
Compliance & ESG
Due diligence, Scope 3 data, diverse/local spend
Measure: audit findings, % spend screened, Scope 3 coverage
Speed & experience
Guided buying, intake-to-PO automation, self-service
Measure: cycle time, % touchless POs, stakeholder NPS

Practical rules that make value credible

  1. Agree a single savings-definition policy with Finance before claiming anything. Separate cost reduction, cost avoidance and cash.
  2. Track value from pipeline to P&L: identified → contracted → realised → booked.
  3. Get involved before the requirement is fixed. Late engagement is the most-cited barrier to value (43% in 2026).
  4. Make one BU sponsor co-own each major category plan, so savings are "their" savings.
03

Procurement as a business unit and as a service

Most large enterprises now run procurement as a hybrid: a small global strategic core, category teams embedded close to the BUs, and a scaled service layer delivered from a GCC/GBS. The choice of model decides who owns budget, who owns savings and what the GCC is allowed to decide.

The three layers of a modern procurement service

Front officeStays close to the business
CPO, category strategy for top categories, BU partnering, supplier relationship management for strategic suppliers.
Middle officeGCC/GBS centre of excellence
Sourcing execution, spend analytics, market intelligence, should-cost, contract management, supplier risk and ESG screening, procurement technology and AI.
Back officeGCC/GBS operations
Supplier master data, requisition-to-PO, catalogue management, invoice exceptions, helpdesk, compliance reporting.

The practical shift in 2026 is that the middle office, not just the back office, is moving to GCCs. That is where alignment problems start.

Five generations of GCC — select one

Setting up (or re-setting) a procurement GCC — practical sequence

  1. Define the mandate. Which S2P processes and which decisions move, and which stay with global or BU procurement. Write it as a decision-rights charter, not an org chart.
  2. Choose the entity and engagement model. Captive subsidiary, build-operate-transfer (BOT) with a partner, or hybrid. Decide transfer-pricing method and safe-harbour vs APA early (Section 9).
  3. Pick the location. Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, NCR remain core; about 10% of GCC units are now in tier-2 cities for cost and attrition reasons. Check state GCC policies for incentives.
  4. Design the service catalogue and SLAs before hiring: services, volumes, quality, turnaround, value KPIs.
  5. Hire leaders first. A GCC procurement head with category depth, plus a data/tech lead. Then category specialists, then operations.
  6. Stabilise, then expand. Transition with documented SOPs and parallel runs; move to judgement-based work only after 2–3 quarters of stable SLAs.
  7. Build the controls. Delegation of authority, segregation of duties, data-access rules and audit trails from day one.
05

How GBS/GCC leaders and global procurement should align

Alignment works when there is one procurement agenda, one value number and clear decision rights, with the GCC treated as part of procurement rather than a supplier to it. In practice this needs five mechanisms.

1 · Mandate & scorecard
The CPO and GCC head sign a joint charter: what the GCC owns, what it supports, and the value targets both are paid on. Avoid the trap where the GCC is measured on cost per FTE and procurement on savings; they will optimise against each other.
2 · Decision rights
A RACI (below) agreed by all parties. Signing authority stays with the contracting legal entity, not the GCC.
3 · Governance rhythm
Monthly service review: SLAs, backlog, quality. Quarterly business review with BU sponsors: value pipeline, risks, capacity. Half-yearly mandate review: which processes or decisions move next, based on maturity evidence.
4 · Talent & careers
Rotations between GCC and onshore category teams, shared capability frameworks, and GCC staff named as category leads for selected global categories.
5 · Data & tech spine
A single supplier master, spend cube and contract repository, owned by one team (usually the GCC), with global procurement as the business owner.

Practical playbook

Run the alignment as a one-year programme with two hard gates; nothing moves to the next phase without the gate evidence.

Days 0–30

Diagnose

  • Map S2P processes and spend
  • Baseline value and SLAs
  • Draft decision-rights charter
  • Legal/tax review of GCC role
Days 31–90

Pilot

  • Sign charter and scorecard
  • Pilot 2–3 categories in GCC
  • Fix supplier master data
  • Approve AI tools and rules
Days 91–365

Scale

  • Scale to tail and indirect
  • GCC leads selected categories
  • Agentic P2P, human sign-off
  • Half-yearly mandate review

CPO, GCC head, Legal, Tax
Gate 2: pilot value proven
Finance-validated savings, SLAs met

Gate 1 is a signed charter reviewed by Legal and Tax; Gate 2 is pilot value validated by Finance, not by procurement.

Legal safeguards

The biggest legal risk is a GCC whose real role has outgrown its paperwork: it negotiates and effectively decides, while its contracts, tax profile and authorities still describe a back office. Supplier contract clause checklist

Owned by Legal, applied by the GCC

Three rules for GCC staff

RULE 1Negotiate within approved mandates and fallback positions; never "agree" outside them, including by email or chat.
RULE 2Do not sign or accept terms on behalf of any group entity unless formally authorised.
RULE 3Escalate any deviation from standard terms to Legal before it reaches the supplier.

Success: outcomes, measurement, review and course-correction

Success means Finance-validated value booked into budgets, faster service for the business and a GCC doing more judgement work, all with a clean legal and audit position. Measure it on one joint scorecard, review it on a fixed rhythm, and use a graded set of interventions when results fall short, from tuning to restructuring.

    When results fall short: diagnose first

    Find where it breaks before changing the structure. Most shortfalls trace to one of six areas.

    MandateAre decision rights clear, and is the GCC actually allowed to decide what it is measured on?
    PeopleDo the leaders and specialists have the category, data and commercial skills? Is attrition eroding capability?
    ProcessAre SOPs, intake and hand-offs clear, or does work bounce between onshore and GCC?
    Data and technologyIs the spend and supplier data trusted? Are tools adopted?
    EngagementDo BUs route demand through procurement early, or bypass it?
    StructureIs the operating model, location, partner mix or legal set-up the real constraint?
